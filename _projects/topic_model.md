#### Objective
Our objective is to identify and extract key themes related to ethical considerations in neuroscience research, as viewed by different stakeholders.

#### Method
Data were collected through loosely structured interviews with various stakeholder groups, including ethicists, neuroscientists, IRB members, Alzheimer's researchers, individuals with mental disorders, and their family members. Participants were asked to discuss important ethical considerations in neuroscience research. Their responses were recorded, transcribed, and processed into text corpora. Preprocessing steps for natural language processing (NLP) included tokenization and scoring using TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency). We then applied Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) to the preprocessed corpora. LDA is a topic modeling technique that assumes a "Bag of Words" model, where documents are treated as unordered collections of words. Each document is considered to be generated by sampling from a distribution of topics, and each topic is composed of a distribution of words. The optimal number of topics (k) was determined based on coherence and exclusivity metrics, which are inversely related. The topics of the chosen model were visualized in a 2D space, and the word distributions for each topic were qualitatively analyzed across stakeholder groups to interpret their meaning.

#### Result
The qualitative analysis revealed no distinct or discernible topics across the stakeholder groups.

#### Discussion
The lack of interpretable results may be attributed to the simplicity of the LDA model, combined with the complexity and variability of the interview data. The unstructured nature of the interviews led to significant divergence in responses, even within the same stakeholder group. Additionally, the sample size was relatively small, which may have limited the model's ability to identify clear patterns. We believe that applying a more advanced algorithm (e.g., GPT-based models) may provide more robust and meaningful insights.
